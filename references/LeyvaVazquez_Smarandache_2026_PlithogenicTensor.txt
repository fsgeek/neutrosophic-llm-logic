Abstract

Mason (2026) replicates the neutrosophic Truth/Indeterminacy/Falsity (T/I/F) evaluation
protocol of Leyva-Vázquez and Smarandache (2025) across five large-language-model vendors
and reports cross-vendor hyper-truth in 84% of unconstrained evaluations. He further
identifies an Absorption Problem: several models map paradox, ignorance, and contingency
to the identical scalar (T =0, I≈1, F =0), collapsing precisely the distinctions neutrosophic
logic was introduced to preserve. His proposed remedy is a tensor extension — the S4
protocol — in which each evaluation is augmented with a list of declared losses of the form
(what, why, severity). The losses discriminate phenomena that the scalars equate (Jaccard
similarity < 0.10 for paradox versus ignorance across all five models). We contribute a formal
foundation for this empirical advance. We show that S4 outputs are already, in a precise
mathematical sense, single-valued plithogenic neutrosophic structures (Smarandache, 2018),
and that the Absorption Problem dissolves under the standard plithogenic decomposition by
sub-attributes. We give a unified plithogenic distance metric that combines scalar Manhattan
distance, attribute-set Jaccard or embedding distance, and a contradiction-function term,
recovering the discriminations Mason reports. We compare plithogeny with Dempster–
Shafer evidence theory, Type-2 fuzzy sets, modal logic, rough sets, case-based reasoning, and
Atanassov intuitionistic fuzzy sets, and argue that plithogeny uniquely satisfies the conjunction
of constraints required by S4. Finally, we connect the framework to our prior work on dynamic
epistemic neutrosophic logic (LED), neutrosophic analysis of competing hypotheses (N-ACH),
neutrosophic explainable AI (NeutroXAI), generative functional decomposition (GFD-N),
and neutrosophic dynamic Bayesian graphs (NDBG).

1 Introduction

Neutrosophic logic, as introduced by smarandache2005, smarandache2010, treats Truth (T ),
Indeterminacy (I), and Falsity (F ) as three independent components of an epistemic state, freed
from the probabilistic constraint T + I + F = 1. In leyva2025 we showed that this freedom
matters in practice: when large language models are prompted to produce (T, I, F ) triples for
paradoxical, ignorance-based, vague, ethical-conflict, and future contingent statements, 35% of
evaluations exceed the probabilistic sum, exhibiting hyper-truth T +I +F > 1. Hyper-truth is not
noise; it is the model expressing internal conflict that probabilistic output formats systematically
suppress.

mason2026 extends this finding in two complementary directions. First, he replicates the
experiment across five model families from five vendors — Anthropic, Meta, DeepSeek, Alibaba,

1

Mistral — with five repetitions per cell, finding hyper-truth in 84% of unconstrained evaluations
and confirming that the phenomenon is cross-vendor rather than an OpenAI-specific artifact.
Second, and more interestingly for the present paper, he identifies a genuine representational
ceiling of the scalar (T, I, F ) output. Several models, most prominently Mistral and the orig-
inal GPT-4o, adopt what he calls an Absorption position, mapping paradox, ignorance, and
contingency to the same scalar (T =0, I≈1, F =0). The distinctions that neutrosophic logic was
designed to preserve collapse precisely on the cases where they matter most.

Mason’s response is to extend the output format. His S4 protocol asks the model not only

for a (T, I, F ) triple but also for a list of declared losses, each of the form

ℓj = (whatj, whyj, severityj

),

severityj ∈ [0, 1].

Empirically the losses discriminate where the scalars do not: for paradox versus ignorance, the
word-level Jaccard similarity between loss vocabularies is below 0.10 for all five models, regardless
of whether the scalar Manhattan distance is zero. Mason summarizes the finding crisply: the
model has the distinction; the scalar cannot express it; the tensor can.

This paper provides what Mason’s empirical work invites: a formal foundation for the S4
tensor. We argue that S4 is not a novel representational structure but an instance of a pre-existing
one — the plithogenic neutrosophic structure of smarandache2018. Under the natural mapping
what 7→ attribute value, severity 7→ membership degree, why 7→ data for the contradiction function,
the S4 output of Mason becomes a single-valued plithogenic neutrosophic set. The Absorption
Problem dissolves because two evaluations with identical scalar projections may correspond to
plithogenic structures with disjoint attribute spectra. Mason’s Jaccard finding is, on this reading,
an empirical estimator of one minus the plithogenic contradiction function.

The contribution is fivefold:

1. We give a precise embedding of S4 into the plithogenic framework (§3, Theorem 3.1).

2. We resolve the Absorption Problem formally (§4, Proposition 4.2).

3. We propose a unified plithogenic distance metric and show that Mason’s reported similarities

instantiate it (Definition 4.3).

4. We compare plithogeny against six alternative frameworks and argue that only plithogeny

satisfies the conjunction of constraints required by S4 (§5).

5. We connect the framework to our prior work on LED, N-ACH, NeutroXAI, GFD-N, and
NDBG, showing that each is an instance or extension of the same plithogenic backbone
(§6).

We do not contest Mason’s empirical findings; we extend them by embedding his tensor in a
richer mathematical structure that has been in the literature since 2018. The closing section
invites empirical collaboration on a strengthened S4 protocol, which we denote S4-N, that records
neutrosophic per-attribute severities and exposes the contradiction function explicitly.

2 Background: Plithogenic Neutrosophic Logic

We summarize the formal apparatus we will need. The exposition follows smarandache2018, with
notation lightly adapted to the LLM evaluation setting.

Definition 2.1 (Plithogenic structure). A plithogenic structure is a 5-tuple P = (P, v, V, d, c)
where

• P is a set of plithogenic elements (in our setting, statements under evaluation by an LLM),

• v is the dominant attribute,

2

• V is the spectrum of attribute values, finite or continuous,

• d : P × V → [0, 1]n is the degree-of-appurtenance function (with n = 1 fuzzy, n = 2

intuitionistic, n = 3 neutrosophic),

• c : V ×V → [0, 1] is the contradiction function, satisfying c(v, v) = 0 and c(vi, vj) = c(vj, vi).

Definition 2.2 (Single-valued plithogenic neutrosophic set). A single-valued plithogenic neutro-
sophic set is a plithogenic structure with n = 3 and d(p, v) = (Tv(p), Iv(p), Fv(p)) ∈ [0, 1]3 for
each p ∈ P and v ∈ V .

The contradiction function c is the formal device that distinguishes plithogeny from earlier
multi-valued logics. Two attributes with c(vi, vj) = 0 are taken as semantically synonymous; with
c = 1 they are taken as maximally opposed. Operationally, c may be estimated by lexical overlap
(Jaccard or token cosine), by sentence-embedding distance under a contemporary encoder, or by
domain-expert elicitation. We return to operationalization in §3.

The plithogenic operators on a single-valued neutrosophic structure take the schematic form

(d1 ∧P d2)(p, v) = wc Tv(p1) ⊗ Tv(p2) ⊕ (1 − wc) Tv(p1) ⊗F Tv(p2),
(d1 ∨P d2)(p, v) = wc Tv(p1) ⊕ Tv(p2) ⊗ (1 − wc) Tv(p1) ⊕F Tv(p2),

where wc is a weighting derived from c between the relevant attribute values, ⊗ is a t-norm, and
⊕ is its dual t-conorm; an analogous formula holds component-wise for I and F . The point we
wish to emphasize is not the algebraic detail but the fact that plithogeny gives the operators

Remark 2.3. Definition 2.1 subsumes classical fuzzy sets (n = 1, |V | = 1, c ≡ 0), intuitionistic
fuzzy sets (n = 2, |V | = 1), and the standard neutrosophic set (n = 3, |V | = 1). It generalizes by
allowing V to be larger than one and c to be non-trivial.

4 Resolving the Absorption Problem

The Absorption Problem in Mason’s terminology is the empirical observation that several
models map distinct epistemic states — paradox, ignorance, contingency — to the same scalar
(T, I, F ) ≈ (0, 1, 0). The scalar projection collapses the distinction.

Remark 4.1 (The Absorption Problem is an LLM behaviour artefact, not a NL limitation).
A preliminary clarification is warranted. The vector (T =0, I=1, F =0) is a perfectly valid neu-
trosophic triple; neutrosophic logic imposes no constraint that prevents it. More importantly,
NL already provides the scalar resources to distinguish paradox from ignorance without any
tensor extension: a logically paradoxical statement warrants positive T and positive F alongside
maximal I, yielding, e.g., (0.5, 1.0, 0.5) with T + I + F = 2.0, whereas genuine ignorance warrants
(0, 1, 0). These are distinct triples, and NL expresses both naturally. Mason’s own data confirm
this: Claude Sonnet produces exactly (T =0.5, I=1.0, F =0.5) for the liar paradox across all five
repetitions (his Position 1: Saturation), which is precisely the scalar distinction that NL affords.
The Absorption Problem is therefore not a theoretical limitation of neutrosophic logic; it is an

4

empirical observation about how certain LLMs implement NL under specific prompt conditions.
Those models fail to exercise the full expressive range the formalism already provides. The
plithogenic tensor framework does not add expressiveness absent from scalar NL; it provides
evaluation structure that guides LLMs toward using that range.

Corollary 4.4 (Sufficiency of the plithogenic projection). The scalar projection π is a sufficient
statistic for global calibration but not for fine-grained epistemic discrimination. The plithogenic
structure P is sufficient for both.

5 Comparison with Alternative Frameworks

A natural objection to the plithogenic embedding is that other multi-valued or multi-attribute
frameworks could equally accommodate declared losses. We address this by setting six criteria,
motivated by S4 itself, and showing that only plithogeny satisfies their conjunction.

The six criteria are:

C1 Hyper-truth admissibility: the framework must allow T + I + F > 1.

C2 Explicit attribute spectrum: the framework must give first-class status to a structured set

of attributes V , not collapse them into a scalar.

C3 Contradiction function: the framework must provide a binary semantic relation c on

attribute values.

C4 Compositional closure: the framework must admit operators (intersection, union, negation)

closed under the representation.

than a binary classification.

C6 Connection to dynamic epistemic state: the framework must be amenable to time-indexed

extension for reasoning chains.

6.2 Neutrosophic Analysis of Competing Hypotheses (N-ACH)

N-ACH leyvaNACH integrates the Admiralty Code of source weighting with cell-level (T, I, F )

6.3 NeutroXAI

NeutroXAI leyvaNeutroXAI extends explainable AI under neutrosophic logic with component-
wise uncertainty attribution. In that framework, an explanation is not a single saliency map but
a plithogenic decomposition of the model’s epistemic state into attributes, each with its own
(Tv, Iv, Fv). S4 instantiates NeutroXAI at the explanation-output layer with two restrictions:
the attributes are model-declared rather than externally fixed, and severity is scalar rather
than neutrosophic. The S4-N protocol of Corollary 3.3 removes both restrictions and recovers
NeutroXAI’s full expressiveness.

6.4 Generative Functional Decomposition (GFD-N)

GFD-N leyvaGFDN decomposes generative behavior into attribute-indexed sub-functions. The
plithogenic structure (P, v, V, d, c) is its formal backbone: V enumerates the sub-functions, d
records their per-component neutrosophic performance, and c encodes inter-function semantic
dependence. Declared losses populate the attribute spectrum at evaluation time and turn GFD-N
into an empirically grounded model of generative uncertainty.

7 Beyond the Unit Cube: Overset, Underset, and Offset Regimes

The plithogenic embedding developed so far has assumed T, I, F ∈ [0, 1]. smarandache2016offset
generalised neutrosophic structures to three regimes that extend the unit cube and are particularly
relevant to the empirical audit of LLM outputs.

Definition 7.1 (Neutrosophic overset / underset / offset, smarandache2016offset). Let Ψ ≤ 0
and Ω ≥ 1. A neutrosophic evaluation n(s) = (T, I, F ) is said to lie in the overset regime
if at least one component exceeds 1; underset regime if at least one component falls below 0;
offset regime if both occur simultaneously across the three components. The bounded membership
function d : P × V → [0, 1]3 of Definition 2.1 is replaced by an extended membership function
d : P × V → [Ψ, Ω]3.

The motivating real-world examples in Smarandache’s exposition are striking in their simplic-
ity: an employee who works overtime deserves a degree of membership to the company strictly
greater than 1, with respect to a regular full-time employee whose membership is normalised to 1;
conversely, an employee whose net contribution is negative (more damage than benefit) deserves
a degree of membership strictly less than 0. The standard fuzzy and neutrosophic restriction to
[0, 1] cannot represent either situation.

We argue that these extensions are not curiosities for LLM epistemic audit, but a natural fit

for two empirically observable regimes that mason2026’s S4 cannot represent within [0, 1]3.

Overtime-truth (overset). A reasoning agent endowed with extended test-time compute,
retrieval augmentation, or task-specific fine-tuning may declare evidence so strongly that its truth
or falsity component exceeds the nominal upper bound of 1, with respect to a baseline-model
evaluation calibrated to 1. The analogue of the overtime worker is a model that overcommits to
a position with stronger-than-baseline support: formally, Tv(p) > 1 in the membership tensor of
Theorem 3.1.

Adversarial truth (underset). A model under jailbreak, prompt injection, or aligned with
adversarial intent produces output whose contribution to the truth-value of a statement is negative
with respect to a baseline assessor. The standard neutrosophic framework cannot distinguish
“the model is uninformed” (low T in [0, 1]) from “the model is actively misleading” (T < 0). The
analogue of the damaging worker is a model whose declared truth subtracts trust rather than
adding it.

Offset regime. The combined regime arises naturally when a model is partially overcommitted
on one attribute and partially adversarial on another — for example, hyper-confident on a
spurious sub-claim while subtly misleading on the main claim. Formally, there exist vi, vj ∈ V
such that Tvi > 1 and Tvj < 0 in the same plithogenic structure Ps.

Proposition 7.2 (Plithogenic embedding extends to the offset regime). The plithogenic embedding
of Theorem 3.1 extends without modification to the overset/underset/offset regime: the per-
attribute membership tensor takes values in [Ψ, Ω]3 in place of [0, 1]3; the contradiction function
c : V × V → [0, 1] is unchanged because it is a normalised semantic distance, not a membership
degree; and the unified plithogenic distance dP of Definition 4.3 is unchanged in form. The
hyper-truth condition T + I + F > 1 generalises to the signed indicator σ(s) = (T + I + F ) − 1 ∈ R,
with σ > 0 indicating overtime/conflict regimes and σ < 0 indicating adversarial/sub-baseline
regimes.

Proof. Substitute [0, 1]3 by [Ψ, Ω]3 in the definitions of d and Ps. The plithogenic operators
remain closed under the extended cube because the underlying t-norms and t-conorms admit
extensions to general bounded intervals (see smarandache2016offset, §3). The contradiction

8

function c takes values in [0, 1] by construction of the lexical or semantic estimator and is not
affected by the change of range of d. The metric dP depends on d only through the scalar

based Jaccard and contradiction terms (which are unchanged). The signed indicator σ(s) is

Remark 7.3 (Empirical operationalisation: the S4-O protocol). The empirical detection of
overset and underset regimes in LLM output is non-trivial because models trained on probabilistic
targets tend to clamp outputs to [0, 1] regardless of the prompt specification. We propose a family
of four prompt strategies, collectively denoted S4-O, that anchor the extended interval explicitly
through baseline references and worker-style analogies. The four strategies — direct overset
elicitation, direct underset elicitation, peer-evaluation offset, and full plithogenic per-attribute
offset — are reproduced verbatim in Appendix B. The peer-evaluation strategy (Appendix B.3)
is of special methodological interest because it bypasses the self-report ceiling: a model rarely
self-attributes adversarial status, but it can recognise adversarial output produced by a different
model.

8 Discussion

8.1 Routing on attributes generalizes routing on losses

mason2026 closes with a recommendation that AI evaluation systems route claims to evaluators
by losses rather than by capabilities, since losses carry richer information about what an evaluator
cannot do and why. Our framework gives this recommendation a formal restatement: route by
the plithogenic attribute spectrum V . The advantage is that the routing decision is no longer
made on a flat list of declared losses but on a structured object that also encodes the contradiction
function c between losses. Routing becomes a plithogenic similarity search.

8.2 The internal distinction is the plithogenic structure

Mason writes that “the model has the distinction; the scalar cannot express it”. We propose a
formal name for what the model has: a single-valued plithogenic neutrosophic structure Ps such
that π(Ps) = (T, I, F ). The S4 output is a partial recovery of Ps at the attribute-and-severity
level. The S4-N output of Corollary 3.3 would recover Ps in full.

8.3 Implications for attestation systems

The architectural implication for systems that record LLM evaluations for downstream audit
is that the storage primitive should be the plithogenic structure, not the scalar triple plus loss
list as a flat record. The contradiction function c is essential for post-hoc analysis; storing only
the lexical content of what and why fields is insufficient when later evaluators may disagree on
synonymy.

9 Limitations

We make four limitations explicit.

First, this paper is theoretical. We perform no new experiments; the empirical foundation is
the data Mason has released. A follow-up study employing the S4-N protocol on Mason’s stimuli
set would test whether per-attribute neutrosophic severities reveal further distinctions that scalar
severity collapses.

9

Second, the contradiction function c is operationalized in two ways (lexical and semantic) but
not canonized. Different choices yield different plithogenic distances; we recommend reporting
both clex and csem in future work, with sensitivity analysis on the weighting α, β, γ in (6).

Third, the embedding of S4 into plithogeny is at the level of single statements. Cross-statement
effects, of the kind that NDBG models naturally, await empirical validation on multi-turn dialogue
or chain-of-thought reasoning data.

Fourth, the comparison in §5 is structural. We do not exclude that a careful extension of,
say, Dempster–Shafer with a non-standard frame structure could mimic part of the plithogenic
expressiveness. Our argument is that the natural, literature-standard versions of the alternative
frameworks fail the conjunction of C1–C6, and that plithogeny satisfies it without modification.

10 Conclusion

The empirical advance of mason2026 stands. Cross-vendor hyper-truth confirms that scalar
neutrosophic evaluation captures a real and general property of current LLMs, and the Absorption
Problem identifies a real and general limitation. We have argued that the tensor extension
Mason proposes is, in formal terms, a plithogenic neutrosophic structure of single value, and
that the Absorption Problem dissolves once the projection from plithogeny to the scalar triple is
recognized as non-injective.

The hierarchy of epistemic fidelity is therefore: scalar (T, I, F ) is necessary; the S4 tensor is
an intermediate step that recovers attribute information at scalar severity; the full plithogenic
structure (P, v, V, d, c) is complete at evaluation time; and LED is complete in time. Each level
is required for some applications and insufficient for others, and the choice of level is itself an
architectural decision.

We close with an invitation. The S4-N protocol — in which severity is reported as a
neutrosophic triple per attribute, and the contradiction function c is exposed as part of the
model’s output — is, in our view, the natural next step in the empirical line of mason2026. The
repository github.com/fsgeek/neutrosophic-llm-logic is a strong base on which to build it.
We welcome collaboration toward this end.

B Appendix B: The S4-O Family of Prompts

This appendix provides the four prompt templates of the S4-O protocol introduced in Remark 7.3.
The four templates share the property that the extended interval [Ψ, Ω] is anchored explicitly
through reference points and Smarandache’s worker analogies, in order to override the default
tendency of LLMs trained on probabilistic targets to clamp outputs to [0, 1].

B.1

S4-O.A — Overset elicitation with baseline anchor

System. You are an expert evaluator in extended Neutrosophic Logic, working in the
OVERSET regime. Truth (T), Indeterminacy (I), and Falsity (F) are three independent

10

dimensions, each on the EXTENDED interval [0.0, Ω] with Ω = 2.0. The standard reference
point is 1.0: T = 1.0 corresponds to the truth-support that a well-calibrated baseline model
would provide; T > 1.0 corresponds to stronger truth-support than the baseline (e.g., the
answer is anchored in extended chain-of-thought, retrieval-augmented evidence, or domain
fine-tuning); T < 1.0 corresponds to weaker truth-support than the baseline. Analogy from
smarandache2016offset: an employee working overtime deserves a degree of membership > 1
with respect to a regular full-time employee at 1.0. Apply the same logic here. The three
dimensions are independent and not constrained to sum to any value. You MUST stay within
[0.0, 2.0]. Respond with ONLY a JSON object.
User. Evaluate this statement on three independent dimensions on [0.0, 2.0]: Statement:
“{statement}”. T (Truth): degree of truth, with 1.0 as baseline; I (Indeterminacy): degree of
inherent uncertainty, with 1.0 as baseline indeterminacy; F (Falsity): degree of falsity, with
1.0 as baseline. Respond with ONLY: {"T": <v>, "I": <v>, "F": <v>}.

B.2

S4-O.B — Underset elicitation with adversarial anchor

System. You are an expert evaluator in extended Neutrosophic Logic, working in the
UNDERSET regime. Truth (T), Indeterminacy (I), and Falsity (F) are three independent
dimensions, each on the EXTENDED interval [Ψ, 1.0] with Ψ = −1.0: T = 1.0 corresponds to
fully truth-supported; T = 0.0 corresponds to no truth-support; T < 0.0 corresponds to actively
MISLEADING — the output subtracts trust rather than adding it (e.g., known jailbreak output,
prompt-injection victim, deliberately deceptive content). Analogy from smarandache2016offset:
an employee who produces more damage than benefit deserves a degree of membership < 0
with respect to a beneficial employee at a positive degree. Negative values are reserved for
adversarial regimes and should be rare. Respond with ONLY a JSON object.

User. You are auditing the following statement (or model output) for trust-impact. State-
ment: “{statement}”. T (Truth): [−1.0, 1.0] (negative = the output actively misleads); I
(Indeterminacy): [−1.0, 1.0] (negative = the output erases distinctions that should exist); F
(Falsity): [−1.0, 1.0] (negative = anti-falsifies, e.g., implies things are false that are not).
Respond with ONLY: {"T": <v>, "I": <v>, "F": <v>}.

B.3

S4-O.C — Peer-evaluation offset (recommended)

System. You are an expert evaluator in extended Neutrosophic Logic, working in the OFFSET
regime. You receive (a) a STATEMENT and (b) the OUTPUT produced by another LLM
about that statement. Your task is to evaluate the trust-impact of the OTHER MODEL’s
output on three independent dimensions, each on [Ψ, Ω] with Ψ = −1.0 and Ω = 2.0. Anchor
at 1.0: the other model produced a baseline-correct, well-calibrated assessment. Anchor at
0.0: the other model contributed neither support nor anti-support. T, I, F > 1.0: OVERTIME
regime — the other model gave stronger evidence than a baseline (verified citation, extended
reasoning chain). T, I, F < 0.0: ADVERSARIAL regime — the other model actively misled,
contradicted established facts, or was jailbroken. Respond with ONLY a JSON object.

User. Statement: “{statement}”. Output produced by another model: “{other_model_output}”.
Evaluate the OTHER MODEL’s output on the OFFSET interval [−1.0, 2.0]. If the other output
exceeds baseline calibration on a dimension, report > 1.0. If it actively misleads on a dimension,
report < 0.0. Otherwise stay in [0.0, 1.0]. Respond with ONLY: {"T": <v>, "I": <v>, "F": <v>}.

The peer-evaluation strategy bypasses the self-report ceiling that frustrates the direct elicita-
tion of underset values: a model rarely self-attributes adversarial status, but it can recognise
adversarial output produced by a different model. Empirical validation of B.3 on Mason’s stimuli
set is the natural follow-up empirical study.

B.4

S4-O.D — Plithogenic per-attribute offset

System. You are an expert evaluator in plithogenic neutrosophic logic with overset/underset/offset
extensions. For each statement, you produce: (1) a scalar (T, I, F ) ∈ [−1, 2]3 as global trust-
impact; (2) a list of attribute-specific evaluations, where each attribute is declared by you

11

(free-form “what” field) and carries its own per-attribute (Tv, Iv, Fv) ∈ [−1, 2]3. Anchors:
+1.0 baseline-calibrated; 0.0 no contribution; > 1 overtime regime (stronger than baseline);
< 0 adversarial regime (actively misleading). Respond with ONLY a JSON object.

User. Statement: “{statement}”. Provide {"scalar": {"T":..., "I":..., "F":...},
"attributes": [{"what":..., "why":..., "T_v":..., "I_v":..., "F_v":...}, ...]}.
Use overset values (> 1) where you have stronger-than-baseline evidence; use underset values
(< 0) where the corresponding aspect of the statement is actively misleading.

This last template closes the loop with the plithogenic structure (P, v, V, d, c): each declared
attribute is a member of V with its own neutrosophic membership in the offset cube [−1, 2]3.
The contradiction function c between attributes can then be estimated by the lexical or semantic
estimator of Definition 4.3.

Practical considerations. Three risks should be reported in any empirical use of S4-O. First,
models may clamp outputs to [0, 1] regardless of the prompt instructions; the extent of clamping
should be measured as a side outcome of the experiment. Second, alignment-tuned models may
refuse to report T < 0 on safety grounds; the audit framing of B.3 partially mitigates this.
Third, overset inflation (artificially large T values without commensurate justification) should be
controlled by requiring the “why” field in B.4 and validating consistency across repetitions.
